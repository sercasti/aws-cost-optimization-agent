# Amazon SageMaker

[Amazon SageMaker](https://aws.amazon.com/sagemaker/)  is a fully managed service that provides every developer and data scientist with the ability to build, train, and deploy machine learning (ML) models quickly. SageMaker removes the heavy lifting from each step of the machine learning process to make it easier to develop high quality models.

If one need to synthesize ML lifecycle in very simple steps, you will have tp:

*   Prepare: Collect and prepare training data.
*   Build: Choose or bring your own ML algorithm.
*   Train and Tune: Set up and manage environments for training; train, debug, and tune models; manage training runs.
*   Deploy and Manage: Deploy model in production; Monitor models; Validate predictions; Scale and manage the production environment.

Amazon SageMaker covers all those aspects and tips are organized accordingly.

When experimenting with the SageMaker SDK, you can use [local mode](https://sagemaker.readthedocs.io/en/stable/overview.html#local-mode)  to train and deploy your model on the notebook instance itself or your local machine, not on managed Amazon SageMaker. All it takes is using the `local` or `local_gpy` instance types. [Chainer](https://github.com/aws/amazon-sagemaker-examples/blob/master/sagemaker-python-sdk/chainer_mnist/chainer_mnist_local_mode.ipynb)  and [Tensorflow](https://github.com/aws/amazon-sagemaker-examples/blob/b573fe59ca26bd24a3c36dd93f78a0516ec2e42c/sagemaker-python-sdk/tensorflow_distributed_mnist/tensorflow_local_mode_mnist.ipynb)  examples.

To do so you can enable “local code” creating a `~/.sagemaker/config.yaml` file that contains:

    1
    2
    local:
      local_code: true

Or you can create a `LocalSession` and configure it directly (works with SageMaker SDK version 2.X):

    1
    2
    3
    4
    5
    6

#### Tip #3: Right-size your Notebook instance

](#tip3)

When

Difficulty

Implementation time

Always

300

You can choose from `ml.t3.medium` to `ml.p3.16xlarge` instance types for your Amazon SageMaker Notebook Instances, across a large set of families. This ranges from $0.0582 to $28.152 per hour in the US East (Ohio) region. You can work on a smaller version your dataset to reduce your need for compute power during exploration phase.

As of today, Amazon SageMaker offers ML compute instances on the following instance families:

*   **T** – General-purpose burstable instances: ideal when you don’t consistently need high levels of CPU, while you can benefit full access to fast CPUs when you need it
*   **M** – General-purpose instances
*   **C** – Compute-optimized instances: ideal for compute bound applications
*   **R** – Memory-optimized instances: ideal for workloads that process large datasets in memory
*   **P**, **G** and **Inf** – Accelerated compute instances: for workloads requiring hardware acceleration
*   **EIA** – Inference acceleration instances: ideal when you need a small fraction of GPU, leverage [Amazon Elastic Inference](https://aws.amazon.com/machine-learning/elastic-inference/) 

Start small, then scale-up.

#### Tip #6: Right-size your training instances

](#tip6)

When

Difficulty

Implementation time

Always

300

minutes

This is not because you will pick the more sophisticated and bigger instance that your training will last less. They may even train slower due to GPU communication overhead!

Start small, pick bigger instance types as required, carefully monitoring your instance load, and parallelize (scale horizontally) if it becomes necessary.

[

##### Trade-offs

](#trade-offs)

You should consider data-parallel distributed training when you cannot fit satisfying batch sizes in one GPU (but can still fit at least a unit batch in a GPU) or if your training time is unacceptably long.

Given the high cost of GPU resources, it is important that the first step you take in your scaling journey is to make sure that you fully leverage a single card (ensure fast data loading, use mixed-precision if possible, no GPU idle time) before scaling to multiple cards architectures. Also, please account that scaling vertically is easier (using bigger instances - even with multiple GPUs); consider scaling horizontally to multiple nodes if using the larger single-machine configurations is not satisfactory. Single-machine training is much easier to write and debug, while communication bandwidth and latencies are better within a single machine than across the network.

Using single instances is the preferred easier solution. We saw several companies trapped in vertical scaling, not being able to move to horizontal scaling. If you choose to scale vertically first, do it knowing the tradeoffs and keeping horizontal scaling in mind.

#### Tip #7: Pack your dataset using RecordIO / TFRecord files

](#tip7)

When

Difficulty

Implementation time

Large dataset made of thousands or millions of files

Large datasets made of thousands or million of files lead to a lot IO. This will slow down your training jobs and will generate additional charges. With [`TFRecord`](https://www.tensorflow.org/guide/datasets#consuming_tfrecord_data)  for TensorFlow, and [`RecordIO`](https://mxnet.incubator.apache.org/versions/master/faq/recordio.html)  for Apache MXNet and most built-in algorithms you can pack several files and their metadata into larger structured files. This will ease dataset manipulation and distribution to training instances.

[

##### Trade-offs

](#trade-offs)

If your dataset is not it that format yet, you will have to convert it - ex. [generate\_cifar10\_tfrecords.py](https://github.com/aws/amazon-sagemaker-examples/blob/master/sagemaker_batch_transform/tensorflow_cifar-10_with_inference_script/generate_cifar10_tfrecords.py) . Viewing that prepared dataset afterward might also be challenging.

[

#### Tip #9: Use Spot Training

When

Difficulty

Implementation time

[Amazon SageMaker](https://aws.amazon.com/sagemaker/)  makes it easy to train machine learning models using managed Amazon EC2 Spot instances. Managed spot training can optimize the cost of training models up to 90% over on-demand instances. Get started with [Managed Spot Training in Amazon SageMaker](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sagemaker/latest/dg/model-managed-spot-training.html) .

You use the `MaxWaitTimeInSeconds` parameter to control the total duration of your training job (actual training time plus waiting time; `MaxWaitTimeInSeconds` must be larger than `MaxRuntimeInSeconds`).

[

##### Trade-offs

](#trade-offs)

Managed Spot Training instances may be reclaimed at any time if AWS needs more capacity. Therefore, managed spot trainings jobs in Amazon SageMaker can be interrupted, causing jobs to take longer to start or finish. You can configure your managed spot training job to [use checkpoints](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sagemaker/latest/dg/model-checkpoints.html) . SageMaker copies checkpoint data from a local path to Amazon S3. When the job is restarted, SageMaker copies the data from Amazon S3 back into the local path. The training then resumes from the last checkpoint instead of restarting.
