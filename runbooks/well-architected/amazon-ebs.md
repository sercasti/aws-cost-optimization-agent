# Amazon EBS

[Amazon Elastic Block Store (EBS)](https://aws.amazon.com/ebs/)  is an easy to use, high performance block storage service designed for use with Amazon Elastic Compute Cloud (EC2) for both throughput and transaction intensive workloads at any scale.

[

#### Tip #1: Delete unused EBS volumes

](#tip-1:-delete-unused-ebs-volumes)

Unused and overlooked Amazon EBS volumes contribute to AWS costs. The lifecycle of EBS volumes can be independent of Amazon EC2 compute instances. Therefore, even if the EC2 instance associated with the EBS volumes is terminated, the EBS volumes tend to persist unless you select the "Delete on Termination" option at launch.

Also, instances spun up and down as part of development and testing cycles may leave orphaned EBS volumes if there are no workflows in use to delete them automatically.

These orphaned EBS volumes accrue charges while unattached.

*   [Controlling your AWS costs by deleting unused Amazon EBS volumes](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/mt/controlling-your-aws-costs-by-deleting-unused-amazon-ebs-volumes/) 

[

#### Tip #2: Right size your EBS

](#tip-2:-right-size-your-ebs)

We often see over provisioned EBS volumes in Amazon EC2 instances. When you start to scale this small amount of unused space start to accumulate and inflate your bills. You should right size your EBS resources as any other resources.

You can monitor how much free space remains in your EBS volumes leveraging [Amazon CloudWatch Agent](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonCloudWatch/latest/monitoring/Install-CloudWatch-Agent.html) .

[

#### Tip #3: Delete unused Snapshot

](#tip-3:-delete-unused-snapshot)

You can back-up your data to Amazon S3 using snapshots. But, snapshots are often forgotten resulting in a $0.05 per GB-Month unwanted additional spending. As part of your financial management, an optimization routine should review all snapshots and delete those that are no longer required.

A no longer required snapshot might be related to older than relevant data or unused AMIs for instance.

You can use [Amazon Data Lifecycle Manager](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSEC2/latest/UserGuide/snapshot-lifecycle.html)  for managing the creation, retention, and deletion of EBS snapshots. In addition to reduce storage costs by deleting outdated backups, you can protect valuable data by enforcing a regular backup schedule. Please read the [Automating Amazon EBS snapshots management using Data Lifecycle Manager](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/storage/automating-amazon-ebs-snapshots-management-using-data-lifecycle-manager/)  blog post for a walk-through.

You might also look at [AWS Backup](https://aws.amazon.com/backup/)  which additionally lets you manage backups over the whole suite of AWS storage and database services.

[

#### Tip #4: Choosing the right EBS volume type

](#tip-4:-choosing-the-right-ebs-volume-type)

Amazon EBS provides several [volume types](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSEC2/latest/UserGuide/ebs-volume-types.html)  and each of those volume types best match certain type of workloads. They differ in performance characteristics and price, so that you can tailor your storage performance and cost to the needs of your applications.

For instance HDD st1 are ideal for Streaming workloads, Big Data, Data Warehouses, Log processing.

*   `st1` is backed by hard disk drives (HDDs) and is ideal for frequently accessed, throughput-intensive workloads with large datasets and large I/O sizes, such as MapReduce, Kafka, log processing, data warehouse, and ETL workloads. These volumes deliver performance, measured in MiB/s of throughput, and include the ability to burst up to 250 MiB/s per TB, with a baseline throughput of 40 MiB/s per TB and a maximum throughput of 500 MiB/s per volume.
*   `st1` is designed to deliver the expected throughput performance 99% of the time and has enough I/O credits to support a full-volume scan at the burst rate.

#### Tip #5: Consider archiving EBS Snapshots

](#tip-5:-consider-archiving-ebs-snapshots)

Archive EBS snapshots that are stored for 90 days or more, but rarely accessed, to save up to 75%.

[

#### Tip #6: Use AWS Compute Optimizer for EBS volume recommendations

](#tip-6:-use-aws-compute-optimizer-for-ebs-volume-recommendations)

AWS Compute Optimizer [supports](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/compute-optimizer/latest/ug/view-ebs-recommendations.html)  IOPS and throughput recommendations for General Purpose (SSD) (gp3) volumes and IOPS recommendations for Provisioned IOPS (io1 and io2) volumes.
